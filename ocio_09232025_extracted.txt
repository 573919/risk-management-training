Overview:
The Enterprise Risk Management Framework defines OCIO’s structured approach to identifying, assessing, mitigating, and escalating risks to strengthen operational resilience. This plan presents a systematic risk management process tailored to OCIO’s unique needs and provides an overview of the risk management model, along with the procedures and processes used to manage risks effectively.
Purpose
The purpose of this Enterprise Risk Management Framework is to establish a comprehensive approach to identifying, assessing, and mitigating risks across OCIO. It is designed to protect sensitive data, ensure service continuity, and maintain compliance with governmental policies and standards. By fostering proactive decision-making, the framework strengthens the security, reliability, and efficiency of OCIO operations while minimizing potential disruptions to government services.
Authority
This framework, authorized by the Office of the Chief Information Officer (OCIO), establishes a shared approach to risk management and empowers designated OCIO federal leadsand stakeholders to carry out these processes in alignment with the guidance outlined in this document.
Scope
This frameworkapplies to all NSF systems, applications, networks, and personnel within the OCIO.

Risk Procedures
The following risk procedure describes the standardized and customized steps to managing risks. The standardized risk procedures should be adhered to across OCIO, while the customized risk procedures are additional procedures required for specific service areas.
Standardized Risk Procedures
Standardized risk proceduresshould be appliedconsistently across the OCIO, encompassing risk identification, assessment, prioritization, development and implementation of response plans,as well as ongoing monitoring and review.
Risk Identification: Identify and pinpoint potential risks using a structured and systematic approach to recognizing where the potential for undesired outcomes or opportunities can arise, such as cyberattacks, hardware failures, software vulnerabilities, or skills gapsthrough audits, threat intelligence, using tools like vulnerability scans, threat modeling, or reviewing past incidents.
Assess: Analyze and evaluate risks considering the causes, sources, probability of the risk occurring, the potential positive or negative outcomes, and then prioritizing the results of the analysis.
Categorize risks by type using the guidance in Appendix A: Risk Types
Define probability and impact using the guidance in Appendix B: Risk Probability Guidelinesand Appendix C: Risk Impact Guidelines, which supports in understanding the risk appetitetoidentify and assess a range of risk response options.
Prioritization: Rank risks based on their severity, focusing on those that could cause significant downtime, data breaches, or compliance violations.
Rank risks using the guidance in Appendix D: Risk Values
Assess risks using the probability and impact to develop a Heat Map. Sample Heat Map in Appendix E: Heat Map Sample
Develop Risk Response Plans by systematically identifying and assessing a range of risk response options guided by risk appetite and creating strategies to address risks:
Mitigate: Develop and implement strategies to reduce or eliminate the impact of identified risks. For example, apply patches, firewalls, encryption, or access controls
Avoid: Prevent the risk from happening by identifying the activity or condition that creates the risk. For example, redesign systems to eliminate vulnerabilities
Transfer: Shift responsibilities for the risk to another party. For example, use cyber insurance or outsource certain functions
Accept: Acknowledge low-priority risks with minimal impact because the risk is within the tolerance level or because the cost of mitigation is deemed too high
Implement Risk Responses: Execute the plans, ensuring actions like software updates, employee training, or backup systems are in place. Assign clear responsibilities and timelines.
Monitor and Review: Continuously monitor the environment using tools like intrusion detection systems or log analysis. Regularly review risk assessments, update controls for new threats.Continuously review established processes to ensure adequate controls are implemented to mitigate risk proactively.
Examples of risk monitoring measures are as follows and additional guidance is in Appendix G: Sample Checklist of Control Categories
Security vulnerability alerts and automated patch management via Renovate
Tracking of CPU, memory, disk usage via AWS CloudWatch
Performance issue alerts via AppDynamics
Auto-correction of misconfigured resources via AWS Config
Anomaly detection & alerts on user behavior via Splunk
Implementation of Zero Trust Architecture for network & identity security
Disaster Recovery
AI-powered risk analytics tools
Mature operational/compliance/security dashboards
Customized Risk Procedures
Customized risk proceduresare essential to describe the detailed risk approach that is specific to each service area (i.e., security).The customized risk procedures should complement while still adhering to the standard risk procedures.
Risk Processes
While the risk procedure describes the steps to managing risks, the risk process describes the process for risk documentation, communication, escalation, training, and reporting.

Training
Risk management training equips professionals with the knowledge and skills to identify, assess, and manage risks effectively, ultimately mitigating potential threats and enhancing organizational resilience. Effective training is the foundation to helping OCIO stakeholders in improved decision making, increased operational efficiency, reduced costs, and improved compliance.
OCIO-wide Enterprise Risk Management trainingis required for key risk roles that cover:
Section 1: Foundation of Risk Management
Section 2: How to Identify Risks
Section 3: Risk Assessment & Prioritization
Section 4: Developing Action/Mitigation Plans
Section 5: Proactive Risk Mitigation through Controls
Section 6: Monitoring, Reporting & Continuous Improvement
Reporting
Risk reporting is crucial for proactive threat detection, informed decision-making, and maintaining compliance. It helps identify potential vulnerabilities and take corrective measures, ultimately minimizing the impact of incidents like cyberattacks and data breaches. Risk reporting shouldinclude all risks across OCIO and shared during various communication channels as described in Risk Escalation section. Risk reporting should also be standardized and automated, where possible.
